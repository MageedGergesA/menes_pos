# Mezze — The Company. A CEO's Plan to $10B by 2035

*Written as the CEO. Forget software. This is how we build the most valuable restaurant software company in the Middle East. Every number is defensible, every claim is grounded in how MENA F&B actually buys, and the strategy is opinionated because a $10B outcome does not come from being agreeable.*

---

## The thesis in one paragraph

We will not get to $10B by selling restaurant software. **No restaurant SaaS company on earth is worth $10B on subscriptions alone** — Toast, a ~120,000-location public company, earns roughly 80% of its revenue from **payments and fintech**, not software licenses. The POS is the *wedge*; the **money flow is the business.** So the plan is simple to state and hard to execute: **use a beautiful, compliance-native POS to become the operating system for MENA restaurants, then own their payments, their working capital, and their supply chain — and take a few basis points of everything a restaurant spends and earns.** We win Egypt on **volume**, Saudi Arabia on the **regulatory mandate**, the UAE on **enterprise**, and the rest of the Gulf on the **flywheel**. We beat the regional incumbent (Foodics) not on features but on **design, compliance depth, payments economics, and the one market they under-serve (Egypt).** That is a $10B company. Here is how.

## Why this is the right decade and the right region

Three tailwinds converge, and they will not repeat:

1. **A forced buying event.** Saudi Arabia's **ZATCA / Fatoora e-invoicing mandate** and **Egypt's ETA e-invoicing** legally *require* restaurants to digitize their sales reporting. This is the single most powerful thing a B2B software company can have: a government making it illegal *not* to buy your category. Every restaurant in Saudi and Egypt must adopt a compliant system — the only question is *whose*.
2. **A cash-to-digital-payments shift.** MENA is moving from cash to cards/wallets fast (mada in Saudi, Meeza/InstaPay in Egypt, wallets across the Gulf). Every one of those transactions is a payment we can process and monetize. The payments TAM is opening exactly as we arrive.
3. **A construction boom in demand.** Saudi Vision 2030 (tourism, entertainment, Giga-projects), UAE's F&B density, and Egypt's massive, formalizing consumer base. The number of restaurants is *growing*, and new restaurants have no incumbent system to rip out — they're greenfield.

**The window is ~2025–2030 to plant the flag, ~2030–2035 to monetize the base.** A latecomer in 2033 faces a market we already own.

## PART 1 — Business Model: layered vertical fintech

Revenue is a stack, and each layer feeds the next. We *land* with software and compliance, we *expand* into payments, and we *monetize* the base with fintech and marketplace.

```
                                   Take rate / margin        Role
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ 5. MARKETPLACE  (app store, integrations)   15–30% of app rev   lock-in  │
 │ 4. FINTECH      (capital, payroll, BNPL,     high margin on     the       │
 │                  insurance, expense cards)    lending spread     multiplier│
 │ 3. PAYMENTS     (card/wallet processing)      0.4–0.8% net GPV   THE money │
 │ 2. MODULES      (inventory, KDS, loyalty,     70–85% gross       expansion │
 │                  delivery, HR, analytics)                                  │
 │ 1. CORE SAAS + COMPLIANCE (POS + ZATCA/ETA)   80% gross          the wedge │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
        LAND ──────────────▶ EXPAND ──────────────▶ MONETIZE
```

- **Layer 1 — Core SaaS + compliance (the wedge).** Cheap, sometimes free, to win the account. Compliance (ZATCA/ETA) is bundled — it's the reason they *have* to say yes. Land-and-expand starts here.
- **Layer 2 — Modules (expansion ARPU).** Inventory, KDS, loyalty, reservations, delivery, HR/scheduling, analytics. Each an upsell; each raises switching cost. This is classic vertical-SaaS net-revenue-expansion.
- **Layer 3 — Payments (the money).** We become the processor (via licensed acquirer partners → eventually our own PayFac/PSP license). Net take rate of ~0.5% on a restaurant's Gross Payment Volume dwarfs the SaaS fee. A restaurant doing SAR 2M/year in card volume at 0.5% net = ~SAR 10,000/year — *3–5x its software fee.* **This is the layer that makes us a $10B company.**
- **Layer 4 — Fintech (the multiplier).** Once we process the money and see the cash flows, we underwrite it: **working-capital advances** (repaid as a % of daily sales — the Toast Capital / Square Capital model), **payroll**, **expense cards**, **BNPL for equipment**, **insurance**. High-margin, data-advantaged, deeply sticky.
- **Layer 5 — Marketplace (ecosystem lock-in).** Third-party apps and integrations, 15–30% revenue share. Small revenue early; enormous *retention* value — a restaurant with 8 integrations never churns.

**Revenue mix trajectory:** SaaS-heavy at launch → payments-dominant by year 3 → fintech-accelerating by year 5. By 2035, ~55–65% of revenue is payments+fintech. That mix is *the* difference between a $1B software company and a $10B fintech.

## PART 2 — Pricing

Pricing is designed to **maximize adoption at Layer 1 and capture value at Layer 3.**

| Plan | Monthly (per location) | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | Free–$29 | Single independent, new opening | POS + compliance + basic reports. *The land.* |
| **Growth** | $79–$149 | Established SMB | + modules (inventory, loyalty, KDS, analytics) |
| **Pro** | $199–$399 | Multi-outlet, high-volume | + delivery, HR, advanced analytics, priority support |
| **Enterprise** | Custom | Chains/franchise (10+) | + multi-entity, SSO, dedicated CSM, SLA |

**But the real price is the payments take rate.** Software is priced *cheap on purpose* — even free at Starter — because a free POS that processes SAR 2M/year at 0.5% earns us more than a $199/month plan. **We give away the software to own the payments.** This is the counterintuitive move that Foodics and the incumbents are structurally slower to make (they price software to profit; we price it to acquire). Hardware is sold near cost or leased — it's a distribution vehicle, not a profit center.

**Guardrail:** payments monetization is transparent and competitive with local acquirers, never predatory. In MENA, reputation and relationships are the moat; a hidden-fee scandal is fatal.

## PART 3 — Sales: a three-engine GTM

One motion does not fit Egypt, Saudi, and enterprise. We run three engines in parallel, weighted differently per market.

1. **Product-led + inside sales (SMB volume — Egypt, Saudi SMB).** Self-serve sign-up, free Starter, compliance as the hook, inside-sales SDRs to convert and upsell to payments. High velocity, low CAC. This is how we win *thousands* of independents cheaply.
2. **Field sales (mid-market & multi-outlet — Saudi, UAE).** Regional reps who know the operators, do on-site demos (design *sells* here), and close 3–20 location groups. Relationship-driven; MENA B2B is a handshake business.
3. **Enterprise & channel (chains, franchises — UAE, Saudi Giga-projects).** Named-account sellers + solutions engineers for the 50–500 location logos; long cycles, high ACV, referenceable brands that pull the rest of the market.

**The compliance trigger shortens every cycle.** "You legally need e-invoicing by [date]" turns a nice-to-have into a deadline. Our SDRs lead with the mandate, not the feature list.

## PART 4 — Marketing: own "compliant + beautiful"

We do not out-spend Foodics on ads. We out-position them.

- **Compliance-led demand gen.** Own every search and every accountant conversation about ZATCA/Fatoora and ETA. Free compliance guides, webinars with tax authorities' timelines, "am I compliant?" tools. Capture the *forced* buyer at the moment of anxiety.
- **Design as brand.** In a category of ugly software, *being beautiful is marketing.* Screenshots sell. We make the product the ad — every demo, every social clip, every walk-past of a Mezze terminal in a café is brand.
- **Arabic-first, locally native.** RTL, Arabic UX, local payment methods front-and-center. We are *of* the region, not a translated import — and we say so loudly.
- **Community + reference.** Restaurant-owner communities, chef ambassadors, "Mezze restaurants" as aspirational. In MENA, operators buy what their peers use.
- **Content + education.** Menu engineering, food-cost, labor — become the *authority* restaurants trust, then sell them the tools.

CAC discipline: PLG + compliance-inbound keeps blended CAC low; payback under ~12 months on the SaaS fee alone, near-immediate once payments attach.

## PART 5 — Implementation & Professional Services

Onboarding is where restaurant SaaS lives or dies — a bad menu build or a failed go-live churns the account before it pays back.

- **Self-serve for Starter** (guided menu import, video, chat).
- **White-glove for Growth+** (a specialist builds the menu, trains staff, does the cash-over go-live) — delivered by our **implementation team early, then by certified partners at scale.**
- **Professional services** for Enterprise (multi-site rollout, data migration, custom integration) — a services P&L that stays ~10–15% of revenue (a services attach, not a services *business* — we're software, not a consultancy; that's the trap we're escaping).
- **Time-to-value target: live in <48 hours for SMB.** Speed of go-live is a competitive weapon.

## PART 6 — Partners, Resellers, Channel

In MENA, **distribution is relationships and local presence** — this is where a foreign entrant fails and a local champion wins.

- **Payment providers & banks.** Partner with the licensed acquirers (mada/Geidea/Network in the Gulf, Paymob/Fawry in Egypt) — then increasingly *become* the merchant-of-record. Banks distribute us to their merchant base; we bring them transactions. **Bank partnerships are the single highest-leverage distribution channel** in the region.
- **Resellers & VARs.** Local IT resellers and hardware distributors who already sell to restaurants — arm them with margin to sell Mezze. Egypt and Saudi run on reseller networks.
- **Accountants & tax consultants.** They advise every restaurant on ZATCA/ETA compliance — make them referral partners with commission. The trusted advisor recommends us at the exact buying moment.
- **Delivery aggregators (Talabat, Jahez, HungerStation, Deliveroo).** Integration partnerships → the restaurant needs us to manage their aggregator chaos.
- **Ghost-kitchen & F&B groups.** Land the operators who run 20 brands from one kitchen.

Channel target: **30–40% of new logos via partners by year 3** — capital-efficient growth we can't hire our way to.

## PART 7 — Marketplace

The marketplace is also how we cover the long tail of features we shouldn't build ourselves — partners fill the gaps, we take a cut, everyone wins, and we stay focused on payments and the core OS.

## PART 8 — International / Regional Expansion (the sequence)

Sequencing is the whole game. Wrong order and we spread thin and die; right order and each market funds the next.

```
 Phase 1 (Y1–2)  EGYPT — volume beachhead
   huge market, ETA mandate, low-cost ops, prove PLG + payments attach cheaply
        │
 Phase 2 (Y2–3)  SAUDI ARABIA — the value engine
   ZATCA mandate = forced adoption, high ARPU, Vision 2030 boom, the profit center
        │
 Phase 3 (Y3–4)  UAE — the enterprise & brand market
   chains, franchises, high-value logos, regional HQ credibility
        │
 Phase 4 (Y4–5)  REST OF GCC + LEVANT + NORTH AFRICA
   Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Jordan; the flywheel spins
        │
```

**Egypt first is the contrarian, correct call.** Foodics is strongest in Saudi; Egypt is a huge, under-served, price-sensitive, ETA-mandated market where our low-cost PLG + payments model fits perfectly and where we can build volume and payments muscle *before* the expensive Saudi land grab. We enter Saudi from a position of scale, not as a startup. **Own the market the incumbent under-serves, then take the market they think is theirs.**

## PART 11 — Customer Success & Support

Once we process a restaurant's payments, **retention is existential** — churn doesn't just lose a subscription, it loses the payment stream and the fintech relationship. Net Revenue Retention is the north-star metric.

- **NRR target ≥120%** — driven by module upsell + payments attach + fintech expansion within the base. The best growth is the base we already own.
- **Gross logo churn <10%/year** at scale — payments + integrations + compliance make leaving genuinely hard.
- **Localized, Arabic-first support**, in-region, fast — restaurants operate at night and on weekends; support must match. Tiered: chat/self-serve for Starter, dedicated CSM for Enterprise.
- **Proactive success**: health scores, usage nudges, "you're leaving money on the table" fintech/upsell prompts. CS is a revenue engine, not a cost center.

## PART 12 — Compliance & Licensing (the regulatory moat)

This is not overhead; **it is the moat.**

- **E-invoicing (ZATCA/Fatoora, ETA).** Deep, certified, always-current integration. This is *why customers buy* and a genuine barrier — it takes competitors real time and relationships to match, and it renews every time the regulation changes (and it will).
- **Payments licensing.** Start as a merchant/partner of licensed acquirers; pursue **PayFac / PSP / e-money licenses** market-by-market (SAMA in Saudi, CBE in Egypt, CBUAE) as volume justifies. Owning the license = owning the economics.
- **Data residency & privacy.** Saudi **PDPL**, UAE data laws, Egypt data rules — in-region data residency (local cloud regions) is increasingly *mandatory* for public-sector-adjacent and enterprise deals.
- **PCI-DSS** via hosted-PSP delegation (SAQ-A path) → full compliance as we take on more of the flow.
- **Financial-services compliance** (KYC/AML) once we lend and issue cards.

The compliance org is a profit protector and a competitive weapon. We staff it early and treat it as strategic.

## PART 14 — The Competitive Moat (why we're not copied)

Ranked by durability:

1. **Payments lock-in.** Once we process the money and run settlement, switching means re-integrating the financial plumbing of the business mid-service. Nobody does that lightly. This is Toast's real moat and it will be ours.
2. **Fintech dependency.** A restaurant with a Mezze working-capital advance and Mezze payroll *cannot* leave. We become their bank.
3. **Compliance depth.** Certified, current, relationship-backed ZATCA/ETA integration is a regulatory barrier that renews with every rule change.
4. **Ecosystem.** Marketplace integrations = accumulating switching cost.
5. **Data.** Every transaction improves our forecasting, underwriting, and product — a compounding advantage new entrants can't bootstrap.
6. **Brand & design.** The wedge and the demo-winner — copyable in theory, hard in practice, and it lowers CAC and churn.
7. **Local presence & relationships.** In MENA, the incumbent with the sales org, the bank partnerships, and the reseller network on the ground wins. Foreign entrants underestimate this every time.

**The compounding logic:** software wins the account → payments monetizes it → fintech locks it → data improves all three → the marketplace and brand widen the lead. Each layer makes the next cheaper and the customer stickier. That is a moat, not a feature list.

## PART 15 — Acquisition Strategy

Buy time, talent, and consolidation — never features we can build.

- **Consolidation (year 3+):** acquire sub-scale regional POS players for their *installed base* and their *local sales orgs* — instant distribution and market share. The MENA POS market will consolidate; we should be the consolidator, not the consolidated.
- **Tuck-ins:** loyalty, reservations, inventory, delivery-integration point solutions — accelerate the module stack.
- **Fintech capability:** a lending/underwriting or payments-tech team to accelerate Layer 4.
- **Acqui-hire:** design and platform-engineering talent, especially in-region.
- **The Foodics question:** either we out-execute and overtake them (base plan), or — if the strategics move — a merger/acquisition consolidates the region into one champion. Both paths lead to the dominant MENA restaurant-OS; we plan for the first and stay open to the second.

- **Primary venue: Tadawul (Saudi Exchange).** Saudi tech listings command premium multiples on Vision-2030 tailwinds and deep local liquidity; a MENA champion listing at home is both *valuation-optimal* and *strategically aligned* with the sovereign capital and regulatory relationships that funded and enabled us. A **dual-listing or later Nasdaq/LSE leg** adds global capital and comps once scale justifies it.
- **Timing: ~2032–2035**, at ~$500M+ revenue with payments+fintech majority and clear operating leverage — the profile public markets reward.
- **Comps to anchor the story:** Toast (payments-led restaurant OS), and regional fintech champions. We IPO as a **vertical fintech**, not a software vendor — that framing is worth billions in multiple.
- **Pre-IPO discipline:** clean SOC2/financial controls, predictable NRR, transparent payments economics, and a governance/board built for public scrutiny from Series B onward.

## PART 17 — The 10-Year Risks (and how we survive them)

A CEO who only sells the upside is lying. The threats that could sink us, and the plan:

1. **Foodics (and incumbents) out-executing / entering Egypt.** *Mitigation:* speed, Egypt-first, design + payments economics they're structurally slow to match, and bank/reseller distribution.
2. **Global entry (Toast/Square into MENA).** *Mitigation:* local compliance, relationships, and payments licenses are years of work for them; we're entrenched before they're serious.
3. **Payments-licensing / regulatory delay.** *Mitigation:* partner-acquirer model first (revenue now), license later (economics later); regional sovereign backing smooths the path.
4. **Capital-market cycles.** *Mitigation:* milestone-gated raises, path to profitability visible by Series C, regional capital less correlated to US cycles.
5. **The design team can't build the platform/commercial company.** *Mitigation:* the CTO's platform-leadership hire *and* a proven regional VP Sales + Head of Payments — the three hires that de-risk the whole plan.
6. **Concentration risk (one mandate, one market).** *Mitigation:* the multi-market sequence; no single regulator or country >50% of revenue by year 5.
